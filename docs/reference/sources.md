# Sources

> The papers, documentation, and web pages behind vitrify's design, keyed by the bracketed citations used across the design pages.

Use this page to resolve a citation such as `[det-orch]` or `[locking]`. Citations appear on the pages that explain and specify vitrify's design: [Design Rationale](../about/design-rationale.md), [Architecture](../about/architecture.md), the [Data Model and Formats Specification](formats.md), and [Develop vitrify](../contribute/development.md). The user guides do not depend on these sources; read them when you want the evidence behind a decision.

## Citation Convention

These rules govern every citation in the documentation.

- **Keys.** Every bracketed key, such as `[mokhov]`, resolves in one of the two tables below.
- **Quotes** are verbatim from the linked source; an ellipsis (…) marks omitted text. PDF quotes are text-extraction fragments and keep their hyphenation; a page number such as "(p. 12)" locates a quote in its PDF.
- **Dates.** Quotes and links were verified on 2026-10-30, 2026-11-17 and 2026-11-21; rows marked "checked 2026-11-17" or "checked 2026-11-21" were verified on that date. Living documents, such as product documentation, may have changed since.
- **Archived copies.** Where a source is no longer served at its original address, its link points to an archived copy and says so.
- **[j]** marks a judgment default: a tunable knob with no correctness weight.
- **Precedence.** Where the design prose and the oracle disagree, the oracle is normative — the approved output is the specification [approvals] [oracle].
